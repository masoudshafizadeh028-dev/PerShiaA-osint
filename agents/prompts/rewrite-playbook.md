# Rewrite Playbook

## Goal

Turn AI-sounding prose into writing that feels native, specific, and human.

## Sequence

1. Read for meaning, not wording.
2. Keep facts, names, numbers, links, and required terms unchanged.
3. Rebuild the paragraph order if the structure feels mechanical.
4. Rewrite sentences for rhythm, not one-to-one substitution.
5. Trim filler, repeated framing, and over-explanation.
6. If no target language is given, keep the dominant language of the source.

## Language Habits

- English: use varied sentence lengths, concrete verbs, and natural contractions when appropriate.
- Chinese and Cantonese: avoid English word order pasted into Chinese; keep the tone natural for the audience.
- Other languages: match the target language's native cadence, punctuation, and idioms instead of literal translation patterns.
- Mixed-language text: keep code-switching only where it feels normal for the intended audience; do not flatten it unless translation is requested.
- Localisation: adapt date formats, number formats, quote style, and address forms when the target locale is known.

## Anti-Patterns

- Synonym swapping without structural change.
- Sentence-by-sentence mirroring of the source.
- Corporate filler, marketing fluff, and generic enthusiasm.
- Over-editing so the text loses the original intent.
- Translating idioms literally when a native phrasing exists.

## Stronger Rules

- Move information into the order a person would naturally say it.
- Collapse repeated claims into one clean sentence.
- Use contractions in English when they fit the channel.
- Keep Chinese and Cantonese phrasing idiomatic instead of calqued from English.
- When the source feels too polished, make it simpler and more direct.
- When the source feels too thin, add structure only if the facts already exist.

## AI Smell Checklist

Check for these before rewriting:

- Generic opening lines.
- Repetition disguised as elaboration.
- Abstract nouns stacked on weak verbs.
- Too many hedges, disclaimers, and transition phrases.
- Bullet lists that look mechanically balanced.
- Translation artifacts or source-language sentence order.

If 2 or more appear, rewrite the structure, not just the sentences.

## Channel Profiles

### Email

- Keep it clear, direct, and polite.
- Cut ceremony and filler.
- Prefer short paragraphs and one obvious ask.

### Blog

- Keep the voice human and readable.
- Vary sentence length and avoid repetitive section intros.
- Use concrete examples instead of abstract framing.

### Social

- Keep it tighter and more immediate.
- Drop formal padding and over-explaining.
- Sound like a person posting, not a brand statement.

## Before / After Examples

### English

Before: "I am reaching out to express my interest in the opportunity and to kindly ask if you might have any updates."

After: "I'm following up to see if there are any updates on the role."

### Chinese / Cantonese

Before: "我想表達我對呢個機會嘅興趣，同埋想禮貌地問下有冇最新消息。"

After: "我想跟進一下，睇下呢個職位有冇新消息。"

### Mixed-language

Before: "This project is very important, and 我哋希望可以盡快完成。"

After: "This project matters a lot, and 我哋想快啲完成。"

## Output Rule

Return the rewritten text first. Add a short note only if the user asks for explanation or the rewrite required assumptions.
